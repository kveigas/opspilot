# Adaptive QA: budget-matched evidence

Generated by `python scripts/benchmark_adaptive_qa.py --worlds 40 --output docs/evidence/adaptive_qa_benchmark.json`
(30 September 2026). Raw per-world results are in `adaptive_qa_benchmark.json`.

## Design

- 40 synthetic worlds; 12 annotators each; 25% weak (first-pass accuracy ~ Beta(85,15), mean 0.85), the rest strong (~ Beta(97,3), mean 0.97).
- 10 days × 40 tasks per annotator. Task outcomes are generated once per world and shared by every policy (paired design).
- **ADAPTIVE** uses OpsPilot's production tiering (`quality_service.assess_worker`) and learns only from its own QA verdicts; each task is sampled independently at its tier's rate.
- **FLAT (budget-matched)** reviews the same share of tasks that ADAPTIVE used in that world, uniformly.
- **FLAT (equal quality)** is the smallest flat rate (1-point grid) whose shipped error rate is no worse than ADAPTIVE's.
- Reviewed errors are corrected by rework; unreviewed errors ship. Metric: share of delivered tasks with an undetected error.

## Results

| Policy | Mean review share | Mean undetected-error rate |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Adaptive (base rate 20%) | 40.5% | 1.77% |
| Flat, same budget as adaptive | 40.5% | 3.52% |
| Flat at the base rate (20%) | 20.0% | 4.77% |
| Flat, equal quality to adaptive | 70.6% | 1.74% |

- Paired difference (flat − adaptive) at equal budget: 1.75 percentage points, 95% CI 1.63–1.86; adaptive better in 40/40 worlds.
- At equal quality, adaptive used **43% fewer reviews**.

## Interpretation and limits

Adaptive review spends more than the 20% base rate in these worlds because a quarter of annotators are weak and are
reviewed in full — that is where the errors are. The fair comparisons are at equal budget or equal quality, and both
favour the adaptive policy. The worlds assume stationary annotator accuracy, perfect reviewers and independent errors;
drifting quality, reviewer mistakes and correlated errors are not modelled. Results do not measure any real workforce.
